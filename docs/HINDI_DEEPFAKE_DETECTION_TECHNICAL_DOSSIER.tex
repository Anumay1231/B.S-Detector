\begin{abstract}
This technical dossier provides an exhaustive specification of every architectural parameter, hyperparameter, dataset partition rule, optimization detail, hardware environment, and empirical finding for the parameter-efficient audio deepfake detection and speaker verification benchmark on low-resource Hindi and Indian-English speech. The deepfake detection system couples a frozen multilingual \textbf{Wav2Vec2 XLS-R 300M} foundation encoder with a trainable \textbf{Sensitive Layer Selection (SLS)} head. We document the scaling trajectory from zero-shot English transfer ($N=0$) through few-shot supervision at $N=10$, $N=50$, and $N=500$ samples per class across three independent deterministic seeds, achieving \textbf{96.83\% Accuracy} and \textbf{2.66\% EER} on SEA-Spoof (peak seed: \textbf{98.00\% Accuracy}, \textbf{2.12\% EER}). Subsequent out-of-distribution augmentation with the multi-speaker, gender-balanced \textbf{IndicSynth} benchmark incorporating timbre-swapping Voice Conversion (\texttt{freevc24}) achieves a peak \textbf{98.75\% Accuracy} and \textbf{1.17\% EER}, completely eliminating voice-cloning vulnerability and reducing gender disparity by 58\%. In addition, to prevent cross-speaker impersonation in shared physical acoustic environments, we adapted the \textbf{ECAPA-TDNN} speaker verification model on 1,865 utterances across 133 Indian speakers with online room acoustic simulation and AAM-Softmax ($s=30, m=0.20$), dropping held-out Hindi clean EER by 66.7\% (from \textbf{4.32\% down to 1.44\%}), boosting accuracy to \textbf{97.14\%}, and slashing same-room cross-speaker impostor similarity from $+0.15$ to $+0.05$.
\end{abstract}

\subsection{Sensitive Layer Selection (SLS) Classifier Head}
The SLS architecture dynamically pools and weights representations from all 24 transformer layers to exploit layer-specific acoustic artifacts \cite{zhang2024sensitive}:

\begin{equation}
\bar{h}_l = \frac{1}{T} \sum_{t=1}^T h_{l,t} \in \mathbb{R}^{1024}, \quad l \in \{1, 2, \dots, 24\}
\end{equation}

\begin{equation}
w_l = \sigma(W_{\text{gate}} \bar{h}_l + b_{\text{gate}}) \in (0, 1), \quad W_{\text{gate}} \in \mathbb{R}^{1 \times 1024}, \quad b_{\text{gate}} \in \mathbb{R}
\end{equation}

\begin{equation}
\tilde{H} = \sum_{l=1}^{24} w_l H_l \in \mathbb{R}^{B \times 1 \times 201 \times 1024}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Audio Conditioning \& Signal Preprocessing}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Input Sampling Rate}: $16,000\text{ Hz}$ (16 kHz mono).
    \item \textbf{Fixed Audio Length}: Exactly $64,600$ samples ($\approx 4.0375\text{ seconds}$).
    \item \textbf{Padding / Truncation Policy}:
    \begin{itemize}
        \item If audio duration $L < 64,600$: repeated and padded to $64,600$ samples.
        \item If audio duration $L > 64,600$: centered symmetrically and cropped to the middle $64,600$ samples.
    \end{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Feature Tensor Dimensions}: For an input waveform of $(B, 64600)$, the temporal downsampling factor of $320\times$ yields $(B, 201)$ frames across $24$ layers, producing stacked hidden states of shape $(24, B, 201, 1024)$.
\end{itemize}

\section{Benchmark Dataset \& Shard Engineering}

\subsection{Corpus Statistics \& Generator Stratification}
The benchmark is constructed from the Hindi split of \texttt{Jack-ppkdczgx/SEA-Spoof} ($54,938$ candidate utterances):
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Candidate Pool}: $16,182$ Bona fide samples, $38,756$ Spoof samples.
    \item \textbf{Held-Out Balanced Test Set ($N_{\text{test}} = 400$)}:
    \begin{itemize}
        \item \textbf{Bona fide Class (200 clips)}: Multi-source balanced between studio acoustic environments (IndicTTS) and in-the-wild crowdsourced speech (Mozilla Common Voice Hindi) to provide robust, realistic acoustic coverage.
        \item \textbf{Spoof Class (200 clips)}: Strictly stratified with exactly 40 clips (20.0\%) from each of \textbf{5 state-of-the-art TTS architectures}:
        \begin{enumerate}
            \item \texttt{edge\_tts}: Microsoft Azure Neural cloud voice synthesizer.
            \item \texttt{vits\_mms}: Meta MMS variational inference end-to-end synthesizer.
            \item \texttt{indic\_tts}: Fast unit-selection and acoustic models from IIT Madras.
            \item \texttt{fastspeech}: Feed-forward non-autoregressive transformer TTS.
            \item \texttt{xtts-v2}: Coqui diffusion/flow-matching zero-shot voice cloning.
        \end{enumerate}
    \end{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Strict Disjointness}: $\text{Test} \cap \text{Train} = \emptyset$ (zero speaker or utterance overlap).
    \item \textbf{Nested Supervision Property}: For each seed $s \in \{0, 1, 2\}$, training sets obey strict subset hierarchy:
    \begin{equation}
    \mathcal{D}_{\text{train}}^{(N=10, s)} \subset \mathcal{D}_{\text{train}}^{(N=50, s)} \subset \mathcal{D}_{\text{train}}^{(N=500, s)}
    \end{equation}
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Parquet Shard Indexing Math}
The SEA-Spoof repository organizes training audio into 30 Parquet files (\texttt{train-00000.parquet} to \texttt{train-00029.parquet}). Shard and row-group boundaries obey strict mathematical determinism:
\begin{equation}
\text{Row Index} = \text{int}(\text{row\_id}.\text{split}(\text{'\_'})[-1])
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
\text{Shard Index} = \lfloor \text{Row Index} / 15,000 \rfloor
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
\text{Row Group Index} = \lfloor (\text{Row Index} \pmod{15,000}) / 200 \rfloor
\end{equation}

Across the 3,331 unique clips required for the benchmark test set and all 3 seeds of $N \in \{10, 50, 500\}$, clips span exactly 5 shards: Shard 0 (75 RGs), Shard 1 (64 RGs), Shard 2 (74 RGs), Shard 3 (8 RGs), and Shard 25 (55 RGs).

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Voice Conversion Vulnerability Remediation}: Prior to adaptation, FreeVC posed the most difficult challenge to the SLS head ($91.49\%$ baseline accuracy) due to its authentic phonetic timing. \subsection{Acoustic Channel Invariance \& Over-the-Air Phone Replay Robustness}
\label{sec:channel_robustness}
In operational deployment, a significant physical vulnerability arises when neural speech synthesis (e.g., ChatGPT Voice, commercial Edge/Azure neural voices) is played aloud via a mobile phone speaker into an external microphone. While direct digital audio achieved over $96.97\%$ accuracy, over-the-air phone speaker replay caused spoof detection accuracy to collapse to $45.95\%$ (worse than random guessing), with commercial neural TTS detection rates dropping to $43.4\%$--$60.0\%$.

\subsubsection{Physical Failure Mode Analysis}
The physical degradation stems from two simultaneous acoustic transformations:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Acoustic Bandpass \& Chassis Resonance}: Physical phone speakers attenuate frequencies below $240\text{ Hz}$ and sharply roll off above $7200\text{ Hz}$, with strong non-linear chassis resonance peaks in the $2.0$--$3.5\text{ kHz}$ region. This bandpass filter eliminates high-frequency neural vocoder phase artifacts ($>8\text{ kHz}$) and low-frequency glottal pulse cues ($<200\text{ Hz}$).
    \item \textbf{Room Impulse Response (RIR) Phase Smearing}: Multipath acoustic reflections in enclosed spaces convolute the waveform with room impulse responses ($RT_{60} \in [0.20, 0.50]\text{ s}$), smearing temporal phase coherence and diluting synthetic speech artifacts into environmental noise ($18$--$30\text{ dB SNR}$).
\end{enumerate}

\subsubsection{Dual-Channel Acoustic Simulation \& Adaptation}

Training was conducted on an NVIDIA L4 GPU with AdamW ($\text{lr} = 2 \times 10^{-5}$) using silence-invariant energy trimming and randomized background room tone beds.

\subsection{ECAPA-TDNN Speaker Verification Adaptation on Indian Speakers}
To prevent spoofed or impostor cross-speaker verification under identical acoustic recording conditions (the "same room, same microphone" threat model), we fine-tuned the ECAPA-TDNN speaker encoder (\texttt{speechbrain/spkrec-ecapa-voxceleb}) \cite{desplanques2020ecapa}. The baseline model, trained predominantly on VoxCeleb1/2 (Western celebrity speech from separate YouTube channels), failed to discriminate Indian speakers recorded in identical reverberant environments, yielding up to $42$--$70\%$ false acceptance.

\subsubsection{Training Formulation \& Acoustic Simulation}
The adaptation was conducted on a speaker-disjoint partition of Common Voice 17 Hindi \cite{ardila2020common} and Google SVQ (Indian English and Hindi) \cite{google2023svq}, comprising 1,865 utterances across 133 Indian speakers ($N_{\text{speakers}}=133$, $\text{half}(\text{client\_id}) = 0$).

To inoculate the embedding manifold against room acoustic overfitting, online simulated room acoustic augmentation was applied with probability $p=0.50$ per sample during training. Each room environment samples:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item A synthetic reverberant impulse response: $h(t) \sim \mathcal{N}(0, 1) \exp(-6.9 t / RT_{60})$ with reverberation time $RT_{60} \in [0.35, 0.8]\text{ s}$ and direct-to-reverberant ratio calibrated to $0.25$.
    \item A second-order Butterworth bandpass equalization filter ($f_{\text{low}} \in [80, 250]\text{ Hz}$, $f_{\text{high}} \in [3500, 7000]\text{ Hz}$) modeling microphone frequency coloration.
    \item An additive environmental noise bed with low-frequency mains hum (50/100 Hz) mixed at SNR $\in [12, 20]\text{ dB}$.
\end{enumerate}

Training utilized Additive Angular Margin Softmax (AAM-Softmax) loss \cite{deng2019arcface} directly optimizing 192-dimensional hyperspherical voiceprint separation:
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}_{\text{AAM}} = -\frac{1}{B}\sum_{i=1}^{B} \log \frac{e^{s(\cos(\theta_{y_i} + m))}}{e^{s(\cos(\theta_{y_i} + m))} + \sum_{j \neq y_i} e^{s \cos \theta_j}}
\end{equation}
with scale parameter $s = 30.0$ and angular margin $m = 0.20\text{ rad}$. The model was trained with AdamW ($\text{lr} = 10^{-4}$, weight decay $10^{-4}$, Cosine Annealing decay to $10^{-6}$) for 15 epochs on an NVIDIA L4 GPU.

\subsection{Saved Model Weights Manifest}
All target checkpoints have been downloaded from the GCP L4 instance into the local repository:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \texttt{models/sls\_head\_channel\_robust.pth} (89 MB): State-of-the-art acoustic channel-robust SLS head fine-tuned with over-the-air phone speaker EQ, room impulse response convolution, and commercial neural TTS ($90.27\%$ Replay Accuracy, $98.48\%$ Clean Accuracy, $5.36\%$ EER).
    \item \texttt{models/sls\_head\_silence\_invariant.pth} (89 MB): Silence-invariant SLS head trained with energy-based active speech trimming and randomized room-tone beds.
    \item \texttt{models/sls\_head\_n500\_indicsynth\_best.pth} (89 MB): State-of-the-art augmented deepfake detection model incorporating Voice Conversion (FreeVC) and gender balancing ($98.75\%$ Accuracy, $1.17\%$ EER).
    \item \texttt{models/ecapa\_indian\_adapted.pth} (80 MB): Indian-adapted ECAPA-TDNN speaker verification model with simulated room acoustic invariance ($1.44\%$ EER on held-out Hindi speech).
    \item \texttt{models/sls\_head\_n500\_seed2\_best.pth} (89 MB): Best performing few-shot base Hindi deepfake model ($98.00\%$ Accuracy, $2.12\%$ EER on SEA-Spoof).
    \item \texttt{models/sls\_head\_n50\_seed2.pth} (89 MB): Intermediate few-shot model ($N=50$).
    \item \texttt{models/sls\_head\_english\_pretrained.pth} (89 MB): Base zero-shot English pretrained checkpoint.
\end{itemize}
